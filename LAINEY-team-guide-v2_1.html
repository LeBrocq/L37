01

What LAINEY is

LAINEY is Latitude 37's knowledge, written down in one place, so that AI reads what we know before it writes anything for us.

It holds our brand story and voice, how we talk about our work, who our clients are, how the business runs, and a defined method for each kind of work we produce. When you ask ChatGPT for a client email or an article, it reads the relevant part of LAINEY first. That is the difference between output that sounds like anyone and output that sounds like us.

LAINEY is not a separate app and there is nothing new to log into. Our knowledge sits on our own network, not inside ChatGPT. We are starting with ChatGPT because it is the right tool now. If something better arrives, we can move to it without rebuilding what we know.

What this is not for

Three things the business is built on are not on the table, and the system has been designed around them rather than into them.

Design judgementTaste, discernment, and knowing what a Latitude 37 home needs to be.
Relationships and trustListening to a client. Holding empathy. Holding a difficult conversation. Being trusted.
AccountabilitySomebody's name is on every decision. That does not change.

AI takes volume. People take judgement. That is the line the whole system is drawn around.

02

The one rule

Only information that could be shared with everyone in the company goes into LAINEY.

Everything else stays where it already lives. That single rule is why LAINEY can be open to the whole team, and why it is safe to connect to AI at all. If you are ever unsure about something, that is the question to ask: could this be sent to everyone at Latitude 37?

Client-specific material never enters LAINEY. No names, no personal details, no contract values. Project codes only. Client records stay in HubSpot, and project documents stay in the project sites where permissions are already enforced.

Inside ChatGPT: Chat and Work

Two spaces, for two different kinds of task. Use the one that fits.

Chat

Quick questions, drafting, rewriting, summarising, thinking out loud. This is most of what you will do.

You collaborating with ChatGPT.

Work

Longer jobs with several steps: comparing a set of documents, building a structured output, working across multiple files at once.

You delegating to ChatGPT.

What it can reach, and what it may do

Read freely. Draft anything. Send, publish or spend, never.

ChatGPT can look across the systems you already have access to and use that context to write. It can never do anything that leaves the building or costs money without a person doing it. That is deliberate, and it is what lets you use it confidently rather than carefully.

SystemWhat AI can doNote

Outlook mailDraft onlyIt writes. You read it and you send it. Always.
Outlook calendarReadMeeting context for briefs and follow ups. No booking, no editing.

It can never see something you could not open yourself. Permissions do the governing. Anything not on this list is not approved yet. If you want a tool added, ask rather than sign up.

Typing client details into a chat: no. LAINEY reading a file you already have permission to open: yes, that is how it is built.

This is the distinction that matters most, and it is the one people most often get tangled in. The restriction is on what you paste, not on what the system reads on your behalf through a connection that IT has set up and that respects your existing access.

06

The red lines

Absolute, and short enough to remember

  • Never paste client identifying detail, pricing, contracts, drawings or specifications into a chat window.
  • AI never approves design, pricing, contract, engineering, safety or employment decisions.
  • Nothing reaches a client, or an audience, without a named person sending it.
  • Approved accounts only. Not your personal one.
  • If it feels risky, stop and ask.

The five checks, before you prompt

  • Am I in an approved tool, on my Latitude 37 account, not a personal one?
  • Am I typing or pasting anything that identifies a client or a project?
  • Am I sharing only what the task actually needs?
  • Will I check the output before it goes anywhere?
  • Am I still the one making the decision?

If something has gone out that should not have, or you think information may have been exposed, report it straight away. Early is always better, and nobody is in trouble for raising it.

07

Verification, and who carries it

Outside the red lines, almost everything you do sits in one zone: allowed, with verification. Drafting, summarising, comparing, structuring, rewriting, researching. AI can research, draft and structure. People interpret, decide and approve.

What verify actually means

Check the facts, the numbers, the dates, the drawing references, the scope, and anything that reads like a commitment.

AI is confident even when it is wrong. That is the failure mode, and it is why verification is the price of admission rather than a nice habit.

Who owns the output

The person who sends it. Not the tool. Not whoever wrote the prompt. Not Steph, and not the system.

That has not changed, and it is not going to. If your name is on it, it is yours, exactly as it was before any of this.

In the February survey, 42 per cent of you named pressure to move faster at the expense of quality as the concern. Verification is the answer to that. Not a promise, a mechanism.

08

Getting a good answer

Most poor output is an underfed prompt rather than a bad tool. Five habits do most of the work.

  1. Say who it is for and what it is forA client update and an internal note are not the same job.
  2. Give it the real source, not your memory of itPaste the document or point it at the file. Do not paraphrase from memory.
  3. Say what good looks like, or show itOne example of something you liked is worth a paragraph of instruction.
  4. Tell it what to leave outCost. Dates. Anything you have not confirmed.
  5. Ask it what it is missing before you ask it to writeThis one habit changes the output more than any other.

Ask for the work by name

There is a defined method behind most of what we produce. Asking for a milestone email, an enquiry response, a defects communication or a suburb guide gets you our process. Asking vaguely gets you a generic version of the same thing.

Start small

Use the smallest relevant extract rather than uploading whole document sets. It is faster, cheaper and more accurate.

What LAINEY is good at today

LAINEY is partly built. Being straight about that is more useful than overselling it, because knowing where it is strong is how you get good work out of it in the first week.

Reliable now

Brand, voice, positioning, messaging and how we talk about our clients. Ask for a client email, an article, a case for something, a piece of website or campaign copy, and it will sound like us.

Not there yet

How the business actually runs. Build process, team, systems, clients, sales process and finance are mostly still empty templates. Ask about those and you will get a confident, generic answer.

Reliable now

The method. There is a defined process for each kind of work we produce, from a milestone email to a subcontractor brief to a monthly report. Ask for the work by name and it follows our method.

Not there yet

Project records. No completed project has been written up, so anything that depends on real project detail, case studies, testimonials, awards, will be thin until they are.

Ready now, whatever your role

  • Writing and rewriting client communication
  • Turning messy notes into a clear structure
  • Comparing documents and finding the gaps
  • Drafting content that sounds like us
  • Summarising, reformatting, sharpening
  • Creating presentations and visualisations

If output comes back generic, it is usually because the part of LAINEY it needed has not been written yet. That is worth reporting, not working around. The operational knowledge is being written over the coming weeks and months, and you will be part of building it.

Starting points, not a list of everything. None of this is the decision. All of it is the work around the decision.

11

How LAINEY gets better

LAINEY does not learn from being used. It gets better when someone deliberately writes down something we know.

That is the only way it improves, and it is why what you notice matters. Using it more does not teach it anything. Telling someone does.

Correct it once, fine. Correct it twice, tell Steph.

The second time you find yourself fixing the same thing, that is not your preference. It is a gap in what Latitude 37 has written down, and everyone else is hitting it too.

What happenedWhat to do
Something works wellTell Steph. If it holds up, it becomes a standard way we do that job, and everyone gets it.
Something went wrongTell Steph. A wrong output is useful information. Nobody is in trouble for finding one.
Output is genericReport it. It usually means something in LAINEY is missing or out of date, and it stays wrong for everyone until someone says so.
It should become company knowledgeSend it through. Only the nominated administrators can publish into LAINEY, which is what keeps it trustworthy.

Who owns what

Steph owns the AI operating system: access, connectors, credits, use cases and problems. One person, not a committee. Kaitie and Lucy are the only people who can update LAINEY knowledge.

Nothing changes in LAINEY without approval, and every change is recorded and reversible. That is what stops it drifting. The system will keep changing as we do, and as the technology does. Nothing you learn now is wasted, but some of it will be superseded.
